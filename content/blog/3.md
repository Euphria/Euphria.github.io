It's been quite a while since my last update. I feel like I haven't had a rest for a long time. Thus I decided to make sure getting some rest this week. This would be a good opportunity to update my blog again. So let me share something during I participated the workshop.

The 3rd Digital Brain Workshop was organized in my university at October 10-12. In the first two days, we had panel dicussion. There was lots of delicious food. I shared my poster during the first panel discussion part. It's seems like they may not be interested in my poster. So I tried to asked others some questions for their poster, talk something about research life, and then promote my poster to them.
That's a little difficult for me to understand Japanese and Japanese-accented English. I am not so good at English or Japanese, but I tried best to commucated with them, not bad.

I also got some goods from Minimax. A T-shirt, a set of pins, a canvas tote bag, a cap. 

Surprisingly, I had the chance to meet Professor Shun-ichi Amari. I often struggle to remember exactly who developed each algorithm. What amazed me most is that he remains active in research to this day. Perhaps 'He is lucky'.

I also met Professor Doya. I have read his book on computational neuroscience. Interestingly, I saw someone walk up directly to Professor Doya and say they wanted to join his group. Maybe one day I will do the same.

![group photo](/3/1.jpg)

After I prepared long time, I shared my poster at IBIS. The poster room was small and packed with people. I thought many people would come to ask me questions since my poster was near the entrance, but that didn’t happen. My supervisor said there was an issue with my title — it might confuse people about what my research is for. As a result, only one person stopped by my poster who was promoted by myself before.

Still, I made some new friends from Institute of Science Tokyo. I also walked around to look at other posters. I noticed that even students from the University of Tokyo and Kyoto University spoke worse English than I did; some could not even introduce their posters in English at all. That was really reassuring.
