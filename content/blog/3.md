It's been quite a while since my last update. I feel like I haven't had a rest for a long time. Thus I decided to make sure getting some rest this week. This would be a good opportunity to update my blog again. So let me share some of my experiences from the workshops and conferences I attended recently.

# The 3rd Digital Brain Workshop
The 3rd Digital Brain Workshop was held at my university from October 10 to 12. During the first two days, we had several panel discussions. There was also lots of delicious food!

I presented my poster during the first poster session. It seemed like people weren't particularly interested in my poster. So I tried asking others questions about their posters, chatting about research and academic life, and then introducing my own poster to them.

It was a little difficult for me to understand Japanese and Japanese-accented English. I'm not particularly good at either English or Japanese, but I tried my best to communicate with everyone. I think it went pretty well!

I also got some merchandise from MiniMax: a T-shirt, a set of pins, a canvas tote bag, and a cap.

Surprisingly, I had the chance to meet Professor Shun-ichi Amari. I often struggle to remember who developed which algorithm, so it was quite an experience to meet someone whose work I've encountered in my studies. What amazed me most was that he is still actively involved in research. Perhaps some people are just built differently.

I also met Professor Doya. I've read his book on computational neuroscience. Interestingly, I saw someone walk straight up to him and say they wanted to join his research group. Maybe one day I'll do the same.

# IBIS
After spending a long time preparing, I finally presented my poster at IBIS. The poster room was small and packed with people. I thought many people would stop by and ask me questions since my poster was near the entrance, but that didn't happen.

My supervisor pointed out an issue with my title — it might have made it difficult for people to understand what my research was actually about. As a result, only one person stopped by my poster, and that was someone I had already talked to and invited over earlier.

Still, I made some new friends from the Institute of Science Tokyo. I also walked around and checked out other posters. I noticed that even some students from the University of Tokyo and Kyoto University struggled with English more than I did. Some couldn't even introduce their posters in English. That was actually pretty reassuring.
